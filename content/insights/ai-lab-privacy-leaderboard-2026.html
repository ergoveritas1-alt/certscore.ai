Findings

01

Cohere led this sample at 100.

Cohere scored 100, with no deductions from the captured visit. Accept, Reject and Options were observed, and no qualifying post-Reject issue was recorded during the eight-second window. Its accessibility coverage was limited, so this score should not be read as a complete accessibility assessment. A score of 100 does not establish legal compliance or cover every future visit.

Anthropic and Hugging Face each scored 93. Both lost four points for accessible names and semantics and three for contrast. Neither received a privacy deduction in these visits. Hugging Face's AWS WAF token was classified as security storage.

02

OpenAI: requests after Reject.

On openai.com, two Cloudflare analytics requests began after Reject was clicked; the first was observed at 0.52 seconds. Under GDPR/ePrivacy logic, a refusal should suppress optional tracking. Request purpose matters: a network request alone does not establish optional tracking or a legal violation. The report retains this observation for review and applies no post-Reject deduction.

OpenAI scored 98. Its only deduction was two points for pointer-target accessibility. No storage deduction was supported by the captured pre-consent inventory. The after-click capture retained 21 requests in total; two were classified as analytics.

03

Mistral: Analytics before a choice, with storage denied.

Mistral scored 82. Its initial Axeptio panel listed Google Analytics 4 and HubSpot with optional switches off. Close was available as a necessary-only equivalent in that state. A Google Analytics request was observed before a choice and carried denied-storage consent signals.

The retained cookie inventory contained Axeptio consent records and preferences, plus the __cf_bm security cookie. These items did not attract a storage deduction. The score reflects eight points for pre-choice Analytics and ten for accessibility findings. This request observation does not establish that Mistral ignored a consent choice, and the scan does not treat disclosure alone as a blanket exemption.

04

DeepMind and Groq retained activity before consent.

DeepMind scored 73. Three cookies were classified as non-essential before consent: YouTube's __Secure-ROLLOUT_TOKEN and the Google Analytics cookies _ga and _ga_LS8HVHCNQ0. The deductions were 14 points for storage, eight for tracking and five for a distinct third-party embed.

Other YouTube cookies, including __Secure-YNID, VISITOR_INFO1_LIVE and __Secure-YEC, remained purpose-review items. Their possible uses do not establish which purpose applied in this visit. YouTube was identified as their provider without treating that attribution as proof of advertising.

Groq scored 81. Its capture included requests classified as Reddit Pixel, Google AdSense and Google Analytics before consent. Its inventory included a PostHog analytics cookie alongside storage whose purposes remained unresolved. The score reflects eight points for storage, eight for tracking and three for contrast. PostHog storage does not by itself establish session recording.

05

Coverage matters alongside the score.

The Meta AI Developer website scored 92, with eight points for unresolved llama_dev_csrf storage purpose. Perplexity scored 88, with eight points for storage purpose review and four for accessible names. Those storage findings call for review; cookie names alone do not establish optional tracking.

xAI scored 92, with eight points for unresolved gb_anon_id storage purpose. Its page document changed during the accessibility check, leaving that part unassessed. The absence of an accessibility deduction is not an accessibility pass. Cohere and xAI had no qualifying post-Reject issue during their eight-second windows.

Methodology

  • We scanned all ten public starting pages through CertScore.ai MCP Light, with tracking_check purpose and EU-Ireland execution, on October 15, 2026 (UTC). The Meta entry covers the Meta AI Developer website at dev.meta.ai. Retained egress evidence verified Ireland for both the consent and runtime visits on all ten sites.
  • The reports cover pre-consent cookies and storage, tracker requests, consent-control observations, eligible Accept and Reject click captures, policy surfaces, transport security and automated accessibility checks. Where Reject could be clicked, the scanner captured roughly eight seconds of after-click activity.
  • The displayed number is the overall score, using overall-posture.v10 and gdpr-eprivacy-posture.v20: a base of 100 minus supported deductions, with family caps. Accessibility affects this number. It is not a standalone GDPR compliance score.
  • After-Reject scoring distinguishes supported optional tracking or contradictions (15 points), sufficiently evidenced storage-denied measurement (five), and performance-only or insufficient-evidence review (zero). Zero deduction does not establish successful refusal or legal compliance.
  • Nine reports completed with limited coverage; the Meta AI Developer report completed without that status. Limits differ by check, from items requiring review to xAI's unassessed accessibility capture. Missing capture, unknown purpose and unsupported paths must be read alongside the score. These are bounded starting-page visits, not full-site audits or assessments of how AI products use conversations or training data.
  • Cookie purpose and provider attribution were assessed separately. Mixed or unresolved purposes remained review items; a provider name was not treated as proof of advertising or other optional use.
  • Perplexity scored 88. Its public-page visit retained storage requiring purpose review and accessible-name findings. The report includes its cookie-choice observations and coverage limits.
