## Existing approach

The established writer is `buildAssessmentBrief` → `buildAssessmentHighlightsPresentation`. It selects already-projected findings, leads with the material observation, preserves its qualifications, adds useful secondary findings when space permits, and supplies the leading finding's next action. The shared limits are 500 characters collapsed and 1,000 expanded. Short results are not padded with routine positive checks or a description of the scanning work. The original 80 reviewed EU/California cases remain unchanged and are checked against the shared regulatory path.

Canonical authority remains verified evidence → normalized concern → concern policy → unified finding/checklist → editorial selection → highlights. Wording changes cannot alter eligibility, priority, score, source/session binding, or the distinction between an observation, a review question and a confirmed failure.

## Recommended shared clarification

Use these decisions consistently for either regulatory view:

| Available canonical output | Write-up |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible priority findings | Keep the existing finding-led writer and ordering. Describe the observation, keep material qualifications, and give the leading next action. |
| No selected priority finding, but source-bound assessed results are available | State one useful assessed result, then one ordered review action, with a supporting check where needed. Keep these results separate from issue count, severity and score. |
| Insufficient, missing or conflicting proof | Use a short truthful fallback. Do not manufacture observations, a pass, a failure or a longer paragraph to fill the space. |

For the assessed-result case:

- Lead with what the assessment established, rather than “we reviewed,” “the scan retained,” or routine coverage totals.
- Preserve material scope in the wording: general consumer language does not become California-specific adequacy; observed storage does not become active use; a click does not become a registered choice.
- Tie the next action to the projected review topic. Recommend checking or reviewing when the conclusion is unresolved; recommend fixing only where the projected finding supports it.
- Give one ordered action, with a secondary verification step if it helps. A long checklist belongs in Detailed evidence.
- Keep useful evidence links and the existing length limits. A positive observation is not padding when it is the substantive result being summarized.
- Require canonical source references and report identity. A provider name, raw policy excerpt, score, screenshot or independent workpaper cannot supply a new executive finding.
- Preserve all existing editorial demotions. An excluded absence or low-priority finding cannot be restored by calling it contextual evidence.

This clarifies narrative treatment; it does not authorize adding facts or actions that lack a canonical projection. Applying the rule to EU reports requires an appropriate already-projected result adapter, not borrowing the California assessment or parsing raw policy text.
