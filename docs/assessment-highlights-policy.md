# CertScore.ai canonical assessment highlights

Current local implementation. Production is unchanged; deployment is not authorized.

## Evidence and narrative authority

Highlights consume already-projected canonical findings or positively assessed results, preserving the verified evidence → normalized concern → concern policy → unified finding/checklist → editorial projection flow. No raw policy text, CMP presence, screenshot, score, click completion or unresolved status may create an executive finding or recommendation.

Findings, qualifications and evidence references retain their original scope. A general consumer clause is not California-specific adequacy; observed storage is not active use; a completed click is not registered consent. Missing or conflicting proof stays unknown. Editorial selection does not change scoring.

## Priority and supporting observations

The shared `assessment-highlight-selection.ts` policy separates priority-list eligibility from narrative eligibility. Stronger findings lead. Explicitly low-severity findings without a high-priority override use the supporting tier in both EU and California views; their existing issue-list eligibility/count is preserved. Existing lower-ranked findings may use remaining narrative space when they add a distinct useful observation; no minimum finding count is imposed.

California collection reviews, including analytics-only collection, are eligible for the top-issue list as qualified privacy-review findings. There is no analytics-only blanket exclusion. Existing catalog priority keeps stronger purpose-qualified GPC findings ahead of collection review. List inclusion does not upgrade `Partial concern`, severity, evidence confidence or a review into a confirmed opt-out failure. Within highlights, the existing complete-fact narrative treatment preserves recipient, timing, processing context and unresolved sale/sharing purpose. This narrative prominence is independent of top-list eligibility and uses no score points.

Per the owner's subsequent instruction, sole non-discrimination disclosure omissions and authorized-agent registration findings remain excluded from both highlights and the top-issue list. They remain in Detailed evidence with their original canonical findings and score effects. Mixed core-rights omissions retain their existing eligibility. The collection finding has explicit California catalog context and cannot enter the EU view through the generic other-findings list.

Supporting additions require a complete observation with its material qualifications; a title alone cannot fill space. Preserve recipient, first-observed timing, processing declarations and unresolved purpose. Preserve the inspected policy scope, applicability assumption and distinction between disclosure absence and actual request handling. Only recognized projected wording may use reviewed concise variants; unfamiliar restrictions remain whole. Existing safe concise variants of stronger findings may remove repeated measurements to make room, while retaining the substantive barrier and its action. Expanded copy preserves facts already shown in collapsed copy and adds supporting breadth before expanding supporting detail.

Agent-registration detail treatment, uncorroborated choice-absence demotion, same-source recipient overlap and combined opt-out grouping remain effective. Exact source/session/document joins remain required for overlap. Scoring, canonical finding eligibility, severity, proof requirements, persisted assessments and detailed evidence are unchanged. No finding becomes urgent merely because it contributes to the score.

## Actions require a supported finding

When an eligible projected finding is selected, the shared writer describes the observed issue or bounded review question, preserves necessary qualifications and uses the finding's applicable next action. This includes a concrete verification step when the finding supports that specific question. A status label alone cannot manufacture a task.

When there is no selected priority finding, source-bound assessment context describes facts only. `AssessmentResultSummary` deliberately has no action field. The shared renderer ignores actions on historical contextual-result objects. Unknown disclosure meaning, applicability or collection-notice matching cannot produce generic instructions to review disclosures, check notices or redo the assessment.

A next step is optional. There is no minimum paragraph length and no obligation to fill the highlights area. Unresolved rows remain in Detailed evidence; they do not become a failed-scan headline, a passing result or a customer task. A neutral navigation link may point to existing evidence without prescribing remediation.

## Shared behavior

Both regulatory views use the shared brief/highlights writer and selection policy. Web executive and regulatory summaries use the same regulatory wrapper. Finding-led results, including eligible supporting observations, take precedence over neutral context. The 430-character collapsed and 1,000-character expanded limits, whole qualifications and evidence links remain in force. Recommendations from omitted supporting observations are not attached to a different displayed finding.

The PDF summary retains its existing additional checklist presentation logic. Its review-only fallback preserves the projected explanation without appending a generic instruction. Supported gap actions remain intact. These source and action rules apply across surfaces without claiming identical web/PDF prose.
