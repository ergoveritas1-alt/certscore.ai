# CertScore.ai California authorized-agent registration policy

The owner authorized local activation of the three-point registration risk
weight on October 25, 2026. No deployment, public capture, scan-model call,
production write or historical report rewrite is authorized.

## Evidence and persistence

Reuse complete, verified, target-owned English policy sources already admitted
by the California review projection. Preserve the existing source hashes,
receipt verification, ownership, translation, completeness and jurisdiction
gates. The existing restriction matcher remains unchanged, including its
4,000-character California scope window, 8 documents of 160,000 characters,
32 candidate inspections, 10 ms cooperative allowance and 2,048-byte research
output. FCP Euro's distant supplement scope remains excluded.

One analysis feeds the optional detached research observer and the positive
registration assessment. Only a completed analysis may create
`california.agent-registration.v1`. Its exact UTF-16 source range, quote,
bounded context range, document URL, text SHA-256, scan ID and bundle SHA-256
must join a complete source document in the parent review. The child has its
own inclusive 2,048-byte UTF-8 cap. It does not assert that all agent instructions
were inspected or that a request would actually be rejected.

Independent Astra review identified two explicit alternative forms missing from
the research matcher: an unregistered natural person may also act as agent, and
the consumer may instead appoint an individual who is not registered. A new
conservative scoring guard holds those forms within the same retained context.
The research matcher and its original outputs remain unchanged. Completed-only
analysis and these additional exclusions are scoring gates, not broader source
eligibility. Unsupported or ambiguous language is not a proven absence; the
bounded matcher is not a complete natural-language legal review.

The optional child is added only to new v10 materializations. Older persisted
reviews without it are never upgraded during scoring or display. Analysis
errors, oversize child output or a failed child provenance join omit only the
new assessment and preserve an otherwise valid California review. Opt-out
verification, request frequency and power-of-attorney restrictions are not
enabled for deductions.

## Canonical scoring and presentation

The child produces `california_agent_registration_required` through normalized
concern, concern policy and unified finding. Concern policy receives the full
validated parent transiently and requires an exact child/source join. The new
normalized concern and dedicated unified detail retain only a bounded compact
projection; its complete envelope, including starting-page URL, is capped at
3,072 UTF-8 bytes. The score independently joins that child back to the retained
full parent. An oversized optional envelope is held without losing the existing
review. Other findings keep their existing evidence contracts. The canonical registry owns the
three-point weight. The new final score version is
`california-regulatory-posture.v22-agent-registration`; the existing parent
policy still selects every other scoring rule, including choice enforcement,
collection, shared notice, GPP and accessibility.

Registration adds at most three once per scan. Subtract already validated
California rights-disclosure and rights-information destination deductions
from that allowance, with a zero floor. Thus existing 3-, 10- or 12-point rights
effects fully absorb registration. Do not change those earlier deductions or
remove the registration finding when its increment is zero. Duplicate passages
and duplicate unified packets do not multiply the effect. Missing, forged or
mismatched required canonical finding proof cannot support a numerical score.

The existing California evidence box includes the factual quote and source;
the already-projected finding remains detailed-only. Customer reports show no
deduction amounts or explanations. The separate internal scoring-review table
uses the registry and latest version. EU and overall scoring remain unchanged.

## Cost and validation

Local validation uses only the 571 retained scans and their original verified
text objects. Independent Astra compares full canonical before/after scores
using actual shared checklist rows, not an empty-checklist shortcut. The frozen
pre-change source snapshot and replay artifacts are under
`artifacts/ca-registration-scoring-2026-10-25/`.

The final compact implementation passed all 571 paired retained replays with
zero errors or limited analyses: 237 sites had evaluable source text, 328 had
no eligible source and six did not reach the observer. It produces six factual
registration findings (1.05% of the cohort), with five new three-point effects
(0.88%). MicroFocus's existing rights deduction absorbs registration. The
California scores change as follows: Anthem 89→86, MicroFocus 94→94, OmniGroup
94→91, UniCourt 74→71, WellnessLiving 96→93 and WOWT 62→59.

All 571 EU scores, shared checklist rows, older deductions, source gates and
availability outcomes match the frozen baseline. The existing 557 available
California scores remain available. Six exact source hashes and UTF-16 quotes
were independently verified. All 571 older field-absent reviews retain their
scores under the new scorer. Fifty existing adversarial wording controls and
four additional alternative/entity controls passed independent scoring checks;
this does not establish a population false-positive rate.

Contracts, focused California scoring/rendering tests and both TypeScript
checks pass. At registration review, the broader surfacing-registry test reported
five missing GDPR narrow policy entries; frozen pre-change code confirmed the
identical failure. The subsequent owner-authorized local registry repair added
explicit entries without changing candidates 1–2 checklist scoring or activating
candidates 3–5; standalone duplicate unified cards remain suppressed. See
`artifacts/gdpr-registry-fix-2026-10-25/` for the independent review and checks.
A scheduling-sensitive scoring fixture uses a deterministic test
clock; the retained replay and dedicated budget checks retain real timing.

The initial generic finding copied full parent evidence and was replaced before
completion. Six-site measurements of the final compact implementation show
12,361–17,287 bytes of modeled retained JSON growth, including existing copies
of the new child. The model covers materialized data, canonical global findings,
normalized concerns and checklist differences; no remote publisher was invoked.

A conservative expected-cost model rounds this to 20 KiB per affected scan and
assumes all 100,000 monthly scans are affected, with 30-day steady retention.
At an illustrative effective $0.20 per incremental logical GB-month, inclusive
of physical database amplification, backups and replication, storage is about
$0.410/month. A 10 ms incremental-processing costing allowance at 4 GB adds about
$0.067 at the prior reference compute rate, for approximately $0.477/month.
The costing allowance includes expected compact validation/serialization; only
the matching engine has the existing cooperative 10 ms limit. Neither is a
worker-memory or capacity change. The retained-source benchmark measured a
0.110 ms median, 0.373 ms p95 and 4.033 ms maximum for analysis plus child building.

This is a conditional expected-cost estimate, not a universal payload/runtime
bound or a current AWS price quote. The six-site maximum is not a bound on every
future report; longer retention, a higher effective storage rate or materially
different payload shapes require a new estimate. No production prevalence is
inferred from the cohort. No additional source retrieval, model tokens, request
count, worker capacity or retention period is introduced. Local validation adds
no recurring service spend; production remains unchanged.
