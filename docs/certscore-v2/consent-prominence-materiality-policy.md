# Consent prominence materiality

Policy: `consent_prominence_materiality.v1` (owner-authorized local implementation, October 13, 2026).

The retained visual comparison identifies styling differences. It is not, by itself, a reason to warn that refusal is materially disadvantaged. Keep the observation and original measurements; do not convert neutral styling into equal-prominence or compliance credit.

The WC01 concern policy requires the existing verified, same-document, paired visual evidence plus an independent geometry corroborator before promoting the prominence concern to a review finding:

- Accept has at least twice Reject's button area.
- Neither Accept dimension is smaller than Reject's corresponding dimension.
- The existing fill or surrounding-emphasis difference is also present.

The area rule is an initial conservative product cutoff, not a regulator-issued standard or a calibrated universal measure of noticeability. It must not be represented as one. Label readability, placement, and other visibility disadvantages are not inferred from fill contrast or missing evidence; using additional corroborators requires appropriate typed retained proof and calibration. No new capture or model work is introduced.

Styling without this corroborator remains an eligible, informational, internal-only normalized concern. It cannot create a unified warning, executive finding, regulatory concern, or score effect. CMP Details consumes that policy result and shows “Visual styling difference observed,” an Informational badge, and the retained comparison. Missing, stale, withheld, unbound, or malformed proof still fails closed.

Implementation follows persisted consent assessment → normalized visual concern → concern policy → unified findings/CMP projection. Historical stored reports are not silently rewritten; explicitly refreshed reports retain the original evidence and apply the versioned interpretation policy. The SITS refresh preserves both action findings and the cookie comparison and removes only the styling-only warning.

Sources: [EDPB Cookie Banner Taskforce, paragraphs 15–19](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-01/edpb_20230118_report_cookie_banner_taskforce_en.pdf) and [CNIL cookie FAQ, question 39](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/FAQ). These provide qualitative guidance and case-by-case assessment, not the numerical product cutoff above.
