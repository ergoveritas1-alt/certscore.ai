# California review capture readiness — local assessment, October 16, 2026

The California Regulatory Risk Review now has six evidence-gated context routes. Five use the existing policy-lane and canonical finding projections; the separately approved bounded sale/sharing observer is configured for every eligible regional sharded scan once the web and scanner changes are deployed. The two new notice/rights-information findings require a visible, accessible, directly linked and classifier-matched surface, plus a retained exact HTTP 404/410 destination. Clearly named privacy-request routes are retained as positive inspection coverage, without a functionality or absence conclusion. No GPC evidence contributes to a customer finding or score.

## What the local historical data can establish

Read-only local `scan_snapshots` audit on October 16, 2026: 960 snapshots dated April 18–October 14; 389 have report projections. Among the 341 snapshots since September 1, 341 have projections, 78 have a retained `privacyAuditEvidence` workpaper, and **zero** have the new typed `consentControlAssessment.californiaPrivacyReview` child. The snapshot-level `california_exposure_likely` field is false across this local history, so it does not define a usable California-eligible denominator. These are historical/local materialization counts, **not** a California detector capture rate or accuracy estimate. The 78/341 workpaper fraction cannot be interpreted as recall or prevalence.

The new assessment has not yet run on a measured California cohort. We cannot claim that California capture is good. There is no evidence-only reviewed corpus for these specific California identities, so precision and recall are also unknown.

## Scoring by review focus

The retained overall page and site score uses the existing GDPR/ePrivacy-weighted rules. California review uses a separate versioned starting-page score from reportable canonical California findings and the supported shared privacy-notice gap. Its policy effects are attached at concern policy, carried through unified findings, and consumed only from persisted projection. A complete named-choice inspection can yield a bounded 100 when no supported deduction is observed; a limited inspection with no supported finding yields no number. Other California obligations, applicability, notice adequacy and rights-method functionality remain outside this score. The report and export show its coverage and state clearly; neither score is a legal compliance grade. The pre-existing draft California evidence-score weights are not production report scoring inputs. Fresh-cohort calibration remains necessary before expanding the rules.

## Latency and cost

The new route extraction uses the already-required rendered DOM read and already-attempted policy fetches. It adds a bounded in-memory filter over at most 1,000 candidates, up to 12 retained request routes and up to eight failed named notice links. It adds no browser session, network fetch, model call, timeout or report-readiness barrier. Estimated incremental compute and storage: **less than $0.50/month at 100,000 scans**, with no new provisioned capacity. This estimate is below the repository's $1/month approval threshold. End-to-end latency has not been benchmarked on a fresh representative cohort, so the expected negligible change is an engineering estimate, not a measured percentile.

The separately approved sale/sharing observer is configured for every eligible regional sharded scan, independent of the Mac mini bot. It uses its bounded action lane and approved $50/month ceiling at 100,000 eligible scans. The last 30 days contained 80,665 invocations of the regional scanner Lambda functions in total, an upper bound on scans because each sharded scan invokes multiple workers. Actual critical-path latency must be measured from terminal lane timing after deployment, not inferred from the 500 ms observation window alone. No production latency increase occurs until deployment.

## Gaps to measure on the first production cohort

Fast static policy coverage can skip rendered link inspection. In those scans, named choice and rights-route coverage remains unknown; an empty workpaper is not absence. The exact-label rights-route classifier intentionally trades recall for precision, and a visible link does not prove a successful request submission. Collection-point notice placement, method functionality, choice symmetry, excessive verification and legal sale/sharing applicability remain unassessed.

The next gate should be a fresh, labeled California cohort with a scan-level denominator and stage counts: verified policy-lane artifact; rendered-inspection attempted/complete/limited/skipped; positive named notice, rights-route and choice observations; exact destination fetch attempted and result; typed child persisted; and final unified finding displayed. Human reviewers should assess a sample of both positives and negatives against retained evidence to estimate precision and recall. First improve classification and evidence retention inside existing visits. Any added rendered visit, deeper rights-flow interaction or extended wait needs a separate latency/cost estimate and owner approval under `AGENTS.md` before implementation.
