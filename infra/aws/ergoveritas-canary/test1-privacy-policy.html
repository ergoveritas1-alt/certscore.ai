Privacy Policy for the ErgoVeritas test 1 canary

Updated 23 October 2026. This notice applies only to ergoveritas.com/test1.html, an owned synthetic scanner-validation page. It describes its deliberately configured behavior so CertScore.ai can test evidence capture. This is not a general privacy notice for other ErgoVeritas pages or a promise that this deliberately failing test is compliant.

Controller and contact

ErgoVeritas Canary Publisher is the controller identified in this synthetic test scenario. The scenario's privacy contact is privacy@test1.example, a reserved, non-deliverable example address for scanner validation, not a working channel for personal data or rights requests. We do not appoint a data protection officer for this synthetic scenario.

Personal data and purposes of processing

We process cookie identifiers, browser and device attributes, page-view events, consent-choice events, and network-request metadata to validate analytics, advertising, session-replay and fingerprinting detection. The purposes of processing are controlled quality assurance, audience-measurement classification and testing whether refusal is reflected in observed requests. The demo enquiry displays name, email, phone, postal code and message fields; its values are not submitted, read by the test runtime, or written to browser storage.

Legal basis for processing

The legal basis described by this synthetic scenario is legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR for controlled diagnostic quality assurance. Optional analytics, advertising and replay are modeled as consent-based processing under Article 6(1)(a) GDPR. The runtime deliberately activates optional requests before a choice and again after Reject, so the stated consent basis does not establish that valid consent was obtained. This notice documents that test defect; it does not justify the behavior.

Recipients and service providers

We share personal data in the form of synthetic cookie identifiers and network-request metadata with analytics providers including Google Analytics, advertising provider Meta, replay provider Microsoft Clarity, and advertising providers Microsoft and Pinterest. AWS is the hosting provider that receives ordinary page-request metadata. The purposes are measurement, advertising classification, session-replay classification and product-event classification. The page uses fixed synthetic identifiers rather than enquiry values; successful provider ingestion is not guaranteed by a request attempt.

Cookies, storage and consent behavior

On a fresh visit the runtime writes _ga_TEST1, _fbp and _clck, plus an OptanonConsent test receipt. It writes only the consent decision marker certscore_test1_consent_state to local storage. These are synthetic identifiers and decision markers, not evidence that a real CMP service has been installed.

Accept and Manage are visible first-layer test controls without implemented decision handlers. Reject changes the test decision to denied and hides the banner, clears the optional cookies, but intentionally recreates _ga_TEST1 and starts a Google advertising-feature collection request approximately 75 milliseconds later. This page does not implement a GPC-dependent reduction. Google, Meta, Microsoft and Pinterest receive advertising event requests carrying fixed synthetic visitor identifiers. A visible Do Not Sell or Share link deliberately returns HTTP 404; no functioning sale/sharing choice is claimed. These behaviors are intentionally left available for independent scanner observation; a visible control or changed receipt alone is not proof of effective refusal.

Data retention

We retain personal data represented by local-storage consent decision markers until you clear this site's browser data. First-party cookie identifiers and the test consent receipt expire within 15 minutes of their latest write. The operator does not receive or retain demo enquiry values. Separate infrastructure logs and third-party recipient retention periods are not specified by this fixture, and no provider-side deletion is asserted.

Data subject rights and withdrawal

You have the right to access your personal data, the right to rectification of inaccurate personal data, the right to erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection to processing based on legitimate interests. Consent may be withdrawn without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing. These are disclosure examples, not working request tools: the reserved privacy contact above does not receive requests. Visitors can clear this site's browser data or close the page to remove local test state or stop further page activity; this does not erase data already received by third parties.

Demo enquiry and collection boundary

The form is for invented personal information only. Its preview button does not serialize fields, send an email, call a CRM, navigate to a collection endpoint or save values. A submit-event guard and a form-action Content Security Policy independently prevent submission. The form inventory demonstrates what information the fields request, not proof that their values were transmitted. Optional requests listed above are separate and use fixed synthetic test identifiers.

Accessibility examples

The test page deliberately includes missing accessible names, an image without an alternative, low-contrast text and an author-declared zoom restriction. These are rendered fixtures for evidence capture.

Concealed-link integrity example

The test page contains one intentionally off-screen outbound link to promotion.example, a reserved example domain. It has no click handler, is not automatically followed and does not collect enquiry values. It is an integrity-review fixture, not evidence that the site was compromised.
