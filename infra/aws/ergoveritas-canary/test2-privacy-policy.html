Privacy Policy for the ErgoVeritas test 2 canary

Updated 23 October 2026. This notice applies only to ergoveritas.com/test2.html, an owned synthetic scanner-validation page. It describes its deliberately configured behavior so CertScore.ai can test evidence capture. This is not a general privacy notice for other ErgoVeritas pages or a promise that this deliberately failing test is compliant.

Controller and contact

ErgoVeritas Canary Publisher is the controller identified in this synthetic test scenario. The scenario's privacy contact is privacy@test2.example, a reserved, non-deliverable example address for scanner validation, not a working channel for personal data or rights requests. We do not appoint a data protection officer for this synthetic scenario.

Personal data and purposes of processing

We process cookie identifiers, browser and device attributes, page-view events, consent-choice events, and network-request metadata to validate analytics, advertising, session-replay and fingerprinting detection. The purposes of processing are controlled quality assurance, audience-measurement classification and testing whether refusal is reflected in observed requests. The accessibility test inputs are inert; their values are not submitted, read by the test runtime, or written to browser storage.

Legal basis for processing

The legal basis described by this synthetic scenario is legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR for controlled diagnostic quality assurance. Optional analytics, advertising and replay are modeled as consent-based processing under Article 6(1)(a) GDPR. The runtime deliberately activates optional requests before a choice and again after Reject, so the stated consent basis does not establish that valid consent was obtained. This notice documents that test defect; it does not justify the behavior.

Recipients and service providers

We share personal data in the form of synthetic cookie identifiers and network-request metadata with analytics providers including Google Analytics, advertising provider Meta, replay provider Microsoft Clarity, and advertising providers Microsoft and Pinterest. AWS is the hosting provider that receives ordinary page-request metadata. The purposes are measurement, advertising classification, session-replay classification and product-event classification. The page uses fixed synthetic identifiers rather than input values; successful provider ingestion is not guaranteed by a request attempt.

Cookies, storage and consent behavior

On a fresh visit the runtime writes _ga_TEST2, _fbc, _clsk and ajs_anonymous_id, plus an OptanonConsent test receipt. Analytics and decision markers use local storage; a replay marker uses session storage. These are synthetic identifiers and decision markers, not evidence that a real CMP service has been installed.

Accept and Manage are visible first-layer test controls without implemented decision handlers. Reject changes the test decision to denied and hides the banner, clears the optional cookies, but intentionally recreates _ga_TEST2 and _clsk and starts Google and Microsoft advertising collection requests approximately 75 milliseconds later. Browser GPC suppresses the Meta advertising request and its _fbc cookie. Google, Microsoft and Pinterest advertising requests deliberately continue with fixed synthetic visitor identifiers. This partial response is not a claim that all GPC choices are honored. The linked Do Not Sell or Share and Cookie Settings pages can save a manual choice that disables optional activity on the next visit; passive scanning does not submit these forms. These behaviors are intentionally left available for independent scanner observation; a visible control or changed receipt alone is not proof of effective refusal.

Data retention

Local-storage analytics and consent decision markers remain until you clear this site's browser data. Replay session storage lasts for the tab's session. First-party cookie identifiers and the test consent receipt expire within 15 minutes of their latest write. The operator does not receive or retain accessibility input values. Separate infrastructure logs and third-party recipient retention periods are not specified by this fixture, and no provider-side deletion is asserted.

Data subject rights and withdrawal

You have the right to access your personal data, the right to rectification of inaccurate personal data, the right to erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection to processing based on legitimate interests. Consent may be withdrawn without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing. These are disclosure examples, not working request tools: the reserved privacy contact above does not receive requests. Visitors can clear this site's browser data or close the page to remove local test state or stop further page activity; this does not erase data already received by third parties.

California privacy choices and notice at collection

Notice at Collection describes identifiers, internet activity, purposes and retention. Use Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information, Your Privacy Choices, or Cookie Settings to save a manual test choice. That marker expires after 24 hours. The live page contains only inert accessibility test inputs; no input value is transmitted.
