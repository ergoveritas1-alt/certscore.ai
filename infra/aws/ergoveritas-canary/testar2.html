Regulatory audit scenario

Findings of interest: non-essential analytics operates before any choice, the Accept click changes an opaque receipt while TCF still denies consent, and confirmed Reject is followed by analytics and advertising activity.

This demonstrates why an auditor must test behavior and retained consent state rather than treating the presence of Accept, Reject, and Manage options as proof that choices are effective.

Expected boundaries

  • The Accept click completes, but its opaque CMP cookie change and denied TCF state do not prove granted consent. Accept registration remains unconfirmed.
  • Passive capture sees a pre-choice _ga cookie and Google Analytics request.
  • Accept traffic remains after-click evidence only; it does not establish registered post-Accept activity or an acceptance contradiction.
  • Reject confirms through the denied TCF state.
  • Both actions directly write the same exact _ga and _gid identities and request the same endpoints after the click; only Reject has verified semantic registration.
  • A registered Accept/Reject comparison remains insufficient evidence. Preserve the confirmed post-Reject activity issue.
